December 06, 2020

CRS-21 MISSION

REWATCH

On Sunday, December 6 at 11:17 a.m. ET, Falcon 9 launched Dragon’s 21st Commercial Resupply Services mission (CRS-21) to the International Space Station from Space Launch Complex 40 (SLC-40) at Cape Canaveral Air Force Station, Florida. Dragon autonomously docked with the space station at 1:40 p.m. ET on Tuesday, December 7.

After 35 days, SpaceX’s Dragon undocked from the orbiting laboratory and successfully returned to Earth, becoming the first cargo spacecraft to splash down off the coast of Florida on Wednesday, January 13 at 8:36 p.m. PT. This mission also marked the completion of the first autonomous undocking of a commercial cargo spacecraft from the International Docking Adaptor.

This was the first flight of the updated cargo version of Dragon, which is capable of carrying about 20 percent more volume than the previous version of Dragon and double the amount of powered locker cargo. The updated vehicle has the capability to allow science payloads to remain inside the spacecraft during the full mission’s duration as an extension to the space station lab space, also known as extend-the-lab. Dragon’s new design also permits for up to five flights to and from the space station, and this cargo version of the spacecraft can stay on station more than twice as long as the previous version.

This was the third flight for the first stage booster supporting this mission, which previously launched Demo-2, ANASIS-II, and a Starlink mission.
