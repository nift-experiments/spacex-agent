Crew-12 Mission

WATCH

On Friday, February 13 at 5:15 a.m. ET, Falcon 9 launched Dragon’s 12th operational human spaceflight mission (Crew-12) to the International Space Station from Launch Complex 39A (LC-39A) at NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida. Dragon autonomously docked with the space station on Saturday, February 14 at 3:15 p.m. ET.

After 234 days docked to the orbiting laboratory, Dragon autonomously undocked at 8:00 a.m. ET on Wednesday, October 7 and successfully returned to Earth, splashing down off the coast of California on Thursday, October 8 at 8:34 a.m. PT.

Aboard the spacecraft were NASA astronauts Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev.

The Dragon spacecraft supporting this mission previously flew the Crew-4, Ax-2, Ax-3, and Crew-9 missions to and from the space station. This was the second flight for the first stage supporting this mission, which previously launched a Starlink mission. Following stage separation, Falcon 9’s first stage landed on Landing Zone 40 (LZ-40) at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station.

During their time on the orbiting laboratory, the crew conducted new research to prepare for human exploration beyond low-Earth orbit and to benefit humanity on Earth.
